      Cell communication

      Rank the ligand–receptor pairs your gene panel can measure or your own list, and how the score is calculated.

      On this page2
      1. Find a pair
      2. Score a pair

      The Cell Comm. tab ranks the ligand–receptor pairs that the gene panel can measure and shows where sending and receiving cells meet in the tissue. It is always in the tab list. It works when gene expression, cell coordinates and a cell-type annotation were mapped at import. Check whether this information was mapped and converted successfully during import; see Importing.

      A ligand is made by a sender cell and a receptor sits on a receiver cell. PointillViz scores how the two sides are expressed and where, but it does not prove that a signal is sent.

      Find a pair

      List

      The list opens with the tab. It needs gene expression, cell coordinates and a cell-type annotation from the import.

      Read the list

      The list holds every ligand–receptor pair that the gene panel can measure. The pairs come from the consensus resource of LIANA+ (built from OmniPath), for mouse or human; which one is used is decided from the capitalisation of the gene names. The line on top counts the pairs, for example 2,414 of 3,989 pairs.

      A row shows ligand → receptor and a bar. Below them stand the sender → receiver cell types that express the pair most specifically. Hovering a row shows, for the ligand and for the receptor, the highest share of expressing cells among the cell types, and the two scores of the pair.

      A protein complex is written with its subunits joined by _, for example Wnt2 → Fzd2_Lrp5. It counts only if every subunit is on the panel.

      When several sections are loaded, a pair needs all its genes in every section. The others are left out and counted in the top line.

      Low expression

      A pair marked Low expression has a ligand or a receptor that is expressed in fewer than 5 % of the cells of every cell type. These pairs are listed after the rest in the default order.

      Filter by gene

      Type a gene or its beginning into Ligand or Receptor. Subunits of a complex match too, and the field suggests names. No match means that no pair fits both fields.

      The list shows 200 pairs at a time; Show more adds 200.

      Sort

      Default puts the most specific pairs first, with Low expression pairs last. Expression puts the most expressed pairs first, whichever cell types express them. Specificity sorts by specificity alone, however few cells express the pair. The bar of each row draws the value that the list is sorted by.

      Score a pair

      Click a pair. The list folds into one line with the pair’s name, Change, which opens the list again, and ×, which clears the pair. Pick a measure below.

      Background

      Paints the mapPlot

      Asks: does the receptor level of a cell follow the ligand level around it?

      Read the result

      Choose Co-variation. The readout gives R, the bivariate Moran’s R of the pair, with z and p.

      R. R summarises, over all cells, whether the receptor level of a cell and the ligand level in its neighbouring cells lie on the same side of their averages. A positive R means mostly the same side, near 0 means no relation, a negative R means opposite sides.

      z. z is R measured against the spread expected without any spatial relation.

      p. p is the two-sided p-value of that z. It is not corrected for the many pairs in the list; a value below 1e-300 is shown as < 1e-300.

      Settings

      Show map paints the pair on the tissue. Colormap picks the palette for the map and the heat map; the bar Map gives the scale, symmetric around 0.

      Map

      Each cell is coloured by how much it supports or opposes the overall score R. Positive values mean that receptor expression in the cell and ligand expression in neighbouring cells deviate from their averages in the same direction; negative values, in opposite directions. Strong colours are large contributions, faint ones small. All values add up to R.

      Hover

      Hovering a cell shows its local R, its value on the map.

      Heat map

      The heat map splits R by cell type. Rows are senders, the cell type of the neighbouring cells; columns are receivers, the cell type of the cell itself. A square adds up the local contributions of that pair of types. Bright squares carry the co-variation, near-black ones work against it. Cells without a cell type are left out. The example is Col4a2 → Itgb5.

      Scale and cell types

      The bar above is the scale, symmetric around 0, drawn with the chosen Colormap. Top 8, Top 16 and All choose how many cell types are drawn, the ones with the largest contributions first. The coloured strips give the colours of the cell types.

      Hover

      Hovering a square names its sender and receiver and gives its value below the heat map. Hovering a row or column label names that sender or receiver.

      Paints the mapPlot

      Asks: how many receptor cells have a ligand cell close by?

      Set the distance

      Choose Proximity. Within sets the distance from 1 to 300 in the dataset’s own units (µm or px; 50 at first). The curve shows the share of receptor-positive cells reached at each distance; drag on the curve or move the slider. The curve marker, readout and heat map update as the distance changes. The cell map does too when shown. All use the distances already calculated.

      Read the result

      The large number below the plot is the share of receptor-positive cells with a ligand-positive cell within the selected distance. In this example, 97.6 % means that 13,979 of 14,327 receptor-positive cells are reached within 50 µm; median 10 µm is their median distance to the nearest ligand-positive cell. A cell is positive when it has at least one count of the gene. There is no p-value.

      Settings

      Show map paints the pair on the tissue and Colormap picks the palette for the map and the heat map. The bar Map runs from 0 to the chosen distance; the closer a cell is to a ligand-positive cell, the stronger its colour.

      Map

      Receptor-positive cells within the distance are coloured by their distance to the nearest ligand-positive cell. All other cells stay grey as tissue context: cells farther away, cells without the receptor, and receptor-positive cells with no ligand-positive cell to measure to. The map follows the slider while it moves.

      Hover

      Hovering a receptor-positive cell shows its nearest ligand distance, also when the cell lies beyond the chosen distance and is grey; none appears if there is no other ligand-positive cell. Other cells show no value.

      Heat map

      The heat map counts the receptor-positive cells that are reached at the chosen distance. Rows are senders, the cell type of the nearest ligand-positive cell; columns are receivers, the cell type of the receptor-positive cell. A square gives the number of such cells, and the bar above is its scale, in cells. The example is Col4a2 → Itgb5 at 50 µm: the bright squares on the diagonal show that, for these cell types, the nearest ligand-positive cell is mostly of the same cell type.

      Change the distance

      The heat map follows Within and the curve. At 10 µm, 39.9 % of the receptor-positive cells are reached; at 50 µm, 100.0 %. The squares brighten as the distance grows. The cell types shown and the colour scale are fixed from the largest distance (300), so the squares change from one distance to the next instead of the whole plot being rescaled.

      Hover

      Hovering a square names its sender and receiver and gives the number of cells below the heat map. Hovering a row or column label names that sender or receiver.

      The list

      Specificity is NATMI’s expression specificity: the ligand’s mean in one sender cell type as a share of its means over the cell types, times the same share of the receptor in one receiver cell type; the best combination is listed. Expression is the geometric mean of the average ligand level and the average receptor level over the cells. Both use log-transformed counts, scaled by each cell’s total. Low expression means that no cell type has 5 % or more of its cells expressing the ligand, or the receptor.

      Co-variation

      Every cell is linked to its six nearest cells in the same section. Ligand and receptor levels are centred on their mean and scaled to length 1. R is the sum over all cells of the cell’s receptor level times the mean ligand level of its neighbours; one cell’s product is its value on the map. z compares R with the spread expected without a spatial relation, and p is the two-sided normal p-value. Nothing is permuted or corrected for the number of pairs.

      Proximity

      A cell is positive for a gene with at least one count. For every cell, the distance to the nearest other ligand-positive cell is found once. The share is the number of receptor-positive cells at most the chosen distance away, divided by all receptor-positive cells; moving the slider only changes this threshold.

      Complexes and fixed settings

      A complex takes the lowest value of its subunits. The tab cannot change the six neighbours, the pair resource, the 5 % limit or the one-count rule, and it analyses all sections of the view together. Scoring a pair is not available for atlases.
