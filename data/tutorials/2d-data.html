A 2D dataset is one tissue section. PointillViz places every cell or spot at the x and y position it was imported with, and the tabs on the left decide what the colours mean: cell types, niches, one gene, a pathway score, another measurement. The tissue stays the same; only its colouring changes.

Extra layers — tissue images, cell boundaries, transcripts — lay over it where the import made them available.

Available tabs depend on the import

Only tabs supported by the imported information are shown. Genes requires expression data, Types a cell-type annotation and Image an image layer. If an expected tab is missing, check whether the required information was mapped and successfully converted during import.

Explore and interact with the tissue

The 2D view is PointillViz's spatial workspace. Here the tissue can be moved, zoomed, fitted to the view and rotated, and hovering a cell shows its annotations and measurements.

The active tab, or a tool's Paint the map, colours the cells by the information you chose. Pin Focus Across Tabs keeps a group of cells highlighted while you switch between views.

Only the view changes, never the dataset.

Understand scale and units

The scale bar

Shows a round length and adapts at every zoom; from 1,000 µm it reads in mm.

The unit comes from the import

µm, mm or pixels: the same unit for the tooltip and every distance the viewer reports.

Nothing is converted

Pixels never become micrometres. A wrong unit is corrected in the Units field at import.

Genes

Paints the mapAnalysis

Appears when gene expression was mapped at import.

A gene can be coloured in three ways, Raw, Density or Multi-RGB; Analysis compares groups of cells.

Pick a mode below to read about it.

Pick a gene

Type into Search gene… and click a row; the list is sorted by the number of expressing cells, and ↑ ↓ step to the neighbouring genes. The Selected gene box gives the number of expressing cells and the maximum; × clears the gene.

The colour bar

The selected gene's expression paints the tissue. The colour bar shows its scale, Colormap its palette, and hovering a cell its value (see Colour by a continuous value above).

Values switches between log1p(counts) and log1p(CP10K), when available; the latter first scales each cell to 10,000 counts. Cells without expression stay dark grey.

Open it

With a gene picked, Open Violin Plot opens a floating window for it. Group expression by chooses Cell Types or Niches; with several niche fields a Niche resolution menu appears. The head counts the cells that express the gene.

Choose the groups

The list shows every group with its colour and cell count; tick the ones to draw. Search filters the list, and Top 20, Select all and Clear selection change the ticks. At first all groups are drawn, or the 20 largest if there are more.

Read a violin

Each violin shows how a group's values spread; cells without the gene sit at the bottom. All violins share one scale, as in the colour bar, and the width shows the shape, not the number of cells.

The box spans the quartiles, the line is the median and the dot the mean. Hover a violin for its numbers.

Order and move

Sort orders the groups by count, median, mean, % expressing or name. Dragging a violin sideways sets a manual order. The window can be moved by its head and resized from its corner.

The colour bar

The selected gene paints a smoothed expression map. The colour bar shows its scale, Colormap its palette, and hovering a cell its value (see Colour by a continuous value above).

Values uses the same expression transformation as Raw. Smoothing radius controls how far each cell contributes to its surroundings, so the colours represent local expression density rather than individual cells alone.

Read the density map

The tissue no longer shows single cells. Each cell that expresses the gene adds its value to its surroundings, as far as the Smoothing radius reaches, and the sum is drawn in the Colormap. Bright areas are where many expressing cells lie close together; areas without them stay dark. Cell size does not apply here.

Three genes at once

Multi-RGB mixes three genes: click R, G or B, then pick a gene for each; the channels add up on the tissue.

Each gene fills one colour channel. A cell's count is log-transformed and divided by the highest value of that gene, so zero is dark and the maximum is full brightness. The three channels add up: where Prl3a1 (red) and Pdia3 (green) are both high, cells turn yellow. Cells with almost no signal in any channel stay dim grey.

Compare two groups

Under Analysis › Differential Analysis, choose a Category (cell types, niches or a custom category), then Group A and Group B, and press Run differential expression.

Read the result

The result lists the significant genes with a volcano plot and split distributions; FDR < and |log2 FC| > filter the list. Click a gene row or a point in the volcano to add that gene to the distributions; ◉ paints it on the tissue.

Run it

Under Analysis › Spatial Analysis, press Calculate spatial gene patterns. The viewer links each cell to its neighbours and scores every gene for spatial organisation (global Moran's I). The result stays while you switch tabs; only the button runs it again.

Read the ranking

The table lists the genes with a significant pattern (q < 0.05), highest Moran's I first. Click a column head to sort, type into Search genes… to filter, and press Show 50 more for the next rows.

Pick a gene

Click a row: the gene is painted on the tissue, and the Selected gene box gives its Moran's I, q-value, rank and share of expressing cells.

Annotation match

Below, the gene's hotspot (the cells where it is high and so are its neighbours) is compared with every annotation. Best match gives the annotation level with its match score, hotspot precision, category coverage and enrichment; Other matches lists further levels.

Click the box or a row to paint that level on the tissue; click it again to return to the gene.

Similar spatial genes

The list shows genes whose hotspots lie in similar places, with similarity and q-value. The first run takes 10–20 seconds. Click a row to preview that gene on the tissue; the analysis stays on the selected gene.

The compare button of a row paints the two hotspots against each other. Back to … returns to the gene.

Lasso

Selection

Always available: press Lasso or P.

Pick a part below to read about it.

Arm the tool

Press Lasso or P. The shape menu opens: Freehand, Rectangular or Circular Selection. While the tool is armed the map is locked, with no pan and no zoom, until a shape is complete.

Press, drag around the cells and release; the outline closes itself. Drag its grip to scale it, or push the outline to reshape that stretch.

Drag from one corner to the opposite one. Each corner handle resizes it, and the opposite corner stays fixed.

Drag from the centre outward. The grip on the right changes the radius, and the centre stays fixed.

Any shape

Drag inside the shape to move it. The numbers update on release, and Escape during a drag undoes it. A drag shorter than 6 px selects nothing. Afterwards pan and zoom work again, and the outline follows the tissue.

Read the region

The selected cells keep their colours, all others turn grey. The Region panel on the right gives the number of cells, their bounding box and the Cell-type composition: the twelve largest types, each with its share of the region and its log2 enrichment against all cells.

Top genes

Top genes (log₂FC region vs other) lists the twelve strongest genes of the region, on log1p(CP10K) or Raw as the switch says; the ? explains the scale. It is a quick read-out over the 600 most expressed genes, not a test: the test is in Diff genes under Compare regions.

Draw region B

Draw region B to compare at the bottom of the Region panel freezes region A (blue). Draw B the same way (orange); it stays editable as under Draw. The bar below counts both regions: Compare regions opens the comparison, Cancel drops B.

Quicker: Cmd/Ctrl+C copies A and Cmd/Ctrl+V pastes it as B, a little shifted, ready to move.

Cell types and Niches

The Region comparison window opens with two bars per cell type, A in blue and B in orange, with their shares, sorted by the largest difference. Niches does the same per niche, with a Resolution menu when there are several; the tab only appears when niches exist.

Diff genes

Opening the tab runs a Wilcoxon rank-sum test on up to 1,000 genes (log1p(CP10K), Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted). The list shows the 15 strongest genes of each side with the log2 fold change and stars for significance.

Volcano plot

Each point is a tested gene: right of zero higher in A, left higher in B; coloured points have p adj < 0.05. A gene expressed in only one region sits in a rail at the side. Hover a point for its values; scroll zooms, drag pans, double-click shows the whole plot. Nothing is painted on the tissue from here.

Gradient

Analysis

Available for 2D data: press Gradient or G. It shows how a feature changes with distance along a line or curve, or outward from a polygon.

Pick a part below to read about it.

Arm the tool

Press Gradient or G (ignored while typing in a field). A floating panel Distance gradient opens with Line, Curve and Polygon. While the tool is armed the map cannot be panned. Switching the geometry clears the drawing.

Press at A, drag and release at B. A new drag replaces the line. The band around it, 10 % of its length on each side, is shaded.

Click points one by one, or press and drag to sketch. Double-click or Enter finishes it, Backspace removes the last point. The path is smoothed afterwards.

Click each vertex and click the first one again, or double-click, to close it. Ten numbered sectors radiate from its centre, and each gets its own profile.

Feature

Feature has a group menu (All features, Genes, Cell types, Niches, Obsm, Continuous) and a search. It opens on the most widely detected gene. For a cell type or niche the value is 1 for its cells and 0 for the rest, so the mean is the share of cells. Only the 200 most detected genes are listed.

Metric, bins, smoothing

Metric shows Mean, % expressing or Both. Bins (8 to 80) divides the distance, Smooth evens the curve out, and Band (2 to 80 %) sets how far from a line or curve cells still count. A polygon uses Direction (Outer or Inner) and Radius instead, and its Band is the width of each sector.

Which cells

Every cell of the dataset in the band counts; filters, the Lasso and visible sections do not change it. Cells without a value are left out. The status line reads N cells in analysis; the profile follows every change of the controls.

Where the profile appears

The profile depends on the geometry drawn. Pick one to read about it.

By default On tissue draws two curves beside the line: the mean in green and the share expressing in orange, each scaled to its own maximum, labelled mean and %expr. In panel draws them as charts under the controls, with the distance from A on the axis.

A curve has no Profile choice: its profile is always drawn in the panel, as the mean in green and the share expressing in orange against the arc length from the start.

Each of the ten sectors is one line with its number as key, the mean solid and the share expressing dashed. On tissue draws them around the polygon, In panel as charts. Outer measures the distance from the edge up to Radius, Inner the way from the edge to the centre in per cent.

Hover

With the chart in the panel, hovering gives the distance range, the values and the cell count of a bin, and the same slice is darkened on the tissue for a line or curve; Arrow keys, Home and End step through the bins. Nothing on the tissue is recoloured.

Close

×, the Gradient button, G or Escape close the tool and discard the shape and the profile. Starting the Lasso closes it too.

Export data

While the tool is open, Export data can save the Distance-gradient profile (one row per bin, per sector for a polygon) and a per-cell Gradient position.

In view counts every cell inside the rectangle on screen, with its share of all cells and a small history of the count. It updates at each frame of a pan or zoom and is exact. Cells dimmed by a legend still count.

Composition

The table follows the annotation painted by the active tab. Each row has the share in view, a bar with a tick for the overall share, log₂ enrichment, z and, with the Loupe, near. Top sets the rows (five by default). Values from fewer than five expected cells are dimmed. The reference is every cell of the dataset, not only the section.

A painted value

When a tab paints a number per cell, such as a gene, the panel adds a histogram of the cells in view against all, with mean, median and > 0 for both.

Loupe

Loupe draws a circle under the cursor, labelled with its radius and cell count. The panel lists its four largest types. The radius follows the zoom until the slider or number box sets a fixed value; the restart button follows the zoom again. It stays where the pointer left the map.

Callouts

Callouts label up to three types on the tissue, those at least 1.5 times over-represented in view with z of 3 or more, as name, fold enrichment and share.

Freeze

Freeze sends all cells in view to the Region panel, as in Read the region, and shows Froze N cells. The region is the whole screen rectangle and does not follow later pan or zoom.

Close and keep

Closing never recolours the cells. Callouts, Loupe and Top are remembered. The Lens has no export; a frozen region can be exported as in Export data.

Gating

Analysis

Enabled when the dataset has at least two channels: genes, continuous columns or matrix channels. It selects cells by two values, as in flow cytometry.

Pick a part below to read about it.

Open and pick channels

Press Gating (no shortcut; also a card in Tools). It is enabled when the dataset has at least two channels in total. X and Y each take a source (a matrix, Gene or Obs), a channel and a transform: linear, log1p or asinh with an editable cofactor. The panel opens with two usable channels already picked.

The density plot

The plot is a 2D histogram of all cells on the two channels, in viridis on a log scale, with 1–N /bin below it. The range is the 0.5th to 99.5th percentile; Full spans every value, and cells beyond the edge sit in the edge bin. Cells with a missing value are left out of every count.

Choose a shape

Pick one; each shape keeps its own gate. Below the plot, number boxes (X min, X max and so on) give exact bounds; empty means open.

Drag on the plot to draw a rectangle, inside it to move it, on a handle to resize it. An edge pushed onto the plot border becomes unbounded. A new gate is the open upper-right corner.

Drag a line of the cross to move one cut, its centre to move both, and click a quadrant to select it. Each corner shows the share of its quadrant.

Click vertices and click the first one, or double-click, to close; at 24 vertices it closes by itself. Then drag a vertex or the inside. Clear starts over, and Escape on the plot drops a draft.

Verdict

The line gives the cells in the gate, their share of the eligible cells and the reference type with its precision (share of the gate) and recall (share of the type that is in the gate), then Cohen's κ. The reference is the type the gate is most about, unless one is pinned.

Types and By

The table lists the six largest types in the gate with share, cells and enrichment (×). Click a row to pin it as the reference, again to release. By opens a list of the share of each niche or section that lies in the gate.

On the tissue

With Tissue on, cells outside the gate fade to 14 % opacity and lose most of their colour; gated cells keep theirs. Hovering a cell adds in gate, outside gate or not gateable with its two values.

Close and remember

× or the Gating button close it; Escape does not. Channels, transforms, shape, gate and pinned type are remembered per dataset for the session. Counts ignore filters and the Lasso. There is no export, and no link to the Lasso.

Explore one hypothesis from multiple angles

One advantage of PointillViz is that the same hypothesis can be followed through different tools and representations.

The following example uses Prl3a1, a placenta-specific prolactin-family gene expressed predominantly by trophoblast cells of the junctional zone, to show how these connected perspectives complement one another and help identify the clearest visualization.

The hypothesis: Prl3a1 marks the junctional-zone trophoblast niche. Press a way to see it.

Paint it

  1. Genes
  2. Prl3a1
  1. Niches
  2. junctional-zone trophoblast niche
  3. Pin Focus Across Tabs
  4. other cells at 5 %

Left, every cell is coloured by its Prl3a1 count on a log scale: dark for none, yellow for the most. Right, the same lobe with one niche kept at full strength and every other niche faded to 5 %. The two patterns lie on top of each other.

Count it per niche

  1. Genes
  2. Prl3a1
  3. Open Violin Plot
  4. grouped by Niches
  5. four largest niches ticked

One violin per niche, log-scaled expression on the y axis, with the quartile box, the median line and the mean dot. In the junctional-zone trophoblast niche 58 % of the cells express the gene and the mean is 9.5 counts; in every other niche fewer than 8 % express it and the mean stays below one count.

Profile the niche

  1. Types
  2. Markers
  3. Group by: Niches
  4. View: Radar profiles
  5. Profiles: four niches

Grouped by niche, the marker profile asks which genes a niche carries. In the junctional-zone trophoblast niche 58 % of the cells express Prl3a1 and 77 % Prl8a6; in the other niches Prl3a1 stays at 8 % or below and Prl8a6 under 25 %. Tpbpa, a general trophoblast gene, is high here (90 %) but also in a second niche (79 %), so the prolactin-family genes are what set this niche apart.

Raise it

  1. Landscape
  2. Color by: Niches
  3. Height by: Prl3a1
  4. height 10×, log1p
  5. drag to tilt

Colour by niche, and raise each cell by its Prl3a1 expression. The green columns of the junctional-zone trophoblast niche stand up out of the tissue; the red niche beside it (M01) rises too, but less, and the rest lies flat.

Keep the strongest cells

  1. Genes
  2. Prl3a1
  3. value window: 40 – 245
  4. Pin Focus Across Tabs
  5. Niches

Under the colour scale, the value window keeps only the cells in a range. Set the lower limit to 40 and 2,395 cells stay lit. Pin that focus, open Niches, and the cells that remain are green: the colour of the junctional-zone trophoblast niche. A window on one gene's scale can be pinned like a category.

Gate it

  1. Gating
  2. X: Prl3a1
  3. Y: Prl8a6
  4. rectangle gate
  5. By: Niche Medium

Gate on two genes at once: pick them for X and Y, keep the box over the cells high in both, and read By niche. 12,213 cells pass (5.2 %). 85 % of them lie in the junctional-zone trophoblast niche: 30 % of that niche is in the gate, 2.7 % of its neighbour M01, and 0 % of every other niche. On the tissue the gated cells trace the same band.

Draw around them

  1. Genes
  2. Prl3a1
  3. Lasso
  4. draw along the expressing cells
  5. Composition by: Niche Medium

Paint the gene, press Lasso and draw by hand around the cells that light up. The Region panel reads what was caught, 26,159 cells, and can group them by niche. 62 % of them belong to the junctional-zone trophoblast niche, four times its share of the whole section; the niche next to it takes most of the rest.

Rank it

  1. Tools
  2. Spatial gene patterns
  3. Calculate
  4. Prl3a1
  5. Annotation match

Spatial gene patterns scores every gene by how strongly its expression clusters in space: Prl3a1 ranks first of 3,255 (Moran's I 0.74). Its hotspot is then compared with every annotation. Best match: the junctional-zone trophoblast niche. 83 % of the hotspot cells lie in it, it covers 54 % of the niche, and the overlap is 5.7 times what chance would give.
