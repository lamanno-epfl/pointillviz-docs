Transcripts draws the detected molecules of chosen genes as a layer of dots over the cells. Each dot is one row of the transcript table: one molecule of one gene, at the position where it was detected. The cells keep their colours; the molecules are not added to them.

A count per cell says how much of a gene a cell holds. The molecules show where that RNA lies: in which part of a cell, along the border between two tissues, or outside any segmented cell.

Up to 12 genes can be drawn at once, each in its own colour. Depth replay shows how the molecules and the counts per cell would look at a lower sequencing depth.

Only the view changes. The transcript table and the cells stay as they were imported.

Where Transcripts appears

The Transcripts tab appears when the dataset holds a transcript table. The Transcripts button in the toolbar is always there but stays grey without a table; the + beside it adds one (see Add a transcript table).

Molecules are drawn only in the 2D spatial view, not in 3D, in Embed or in the Expression Landscape.

The pictures on this page show a Xenium section of the mouse small intestine, rolled up as a Swiss roll: 215,195 cells and 87.4 million molecules. Two genes mark the two ends of the crypt–villus axis: Defa5 (green), made by Paneth cells at the base of the crypts, and Apoa4 (magenta), made by enterocytes on the villi.

Add a transcript table

A table can be added during import or later to an open dataset. Both ways ask for the same fields; only the place where they start differs.

Turn on the tile.

In the mapping step of the import wizard, press the Transcripts tile among the optional fields, as described on the Importing page. Enter the server path of the table and press Inspect.

Imports with several sections.

A transcript table has one coordinate frame. When the import converts several files, or a section column lays several sections out side by side, the tile refuses the table. Import that section on its own to add its table.

Open the sheet.

Press + beside Transcripts in the toolbar, enter the server path of the table and press Inspect.

A dataset holds one table. When it already has one, the sheet is called Replace transcripts, and the new table replaces the old one.

Add the table.

Press Add transcripts. The table is converted on the server; the sheet may be closed meanwhile, and the + fills as a ring until the work ends. The Transcripts tab then appears without a reload, with Show all transcripts on.

Where it is refused.

The + is for a 2D dataset with one section. Atlases, 3D data and sections laid out side by side are refused, and the sheet gives the reason.

After Inspect, both show the same fields.

The table.

One row per detected molecule, as .parquet, .csv or .csv.gz, such as Xenium transcripts.parquet, MERSCOPE detected_transcripts.csv or CosMx *_tx_file.csv.gz. A table with one row per cell or per gene is not a transcript table.

The columns.

Inspect reads the column names and proposes the x, y and Gene columns; Quality (QV) and Cell id are optional. Xenium, MERSCOPE, CosMx and plain x, y and gene names are recognised, and every menu can be changed. x, y and QV must be numeric.

Scale.

Scale multiplies the table's x and y into the cells' coordinates; 1 keeps them as they are. There is no offset: the table must start from the same origin as the cells.

When the two ranges differ, the sheet says so and offers Use scale with the ratio of the ranges.

Check the suggestion.

The ratio is a hint, not a reading of the units. The table in the picture has no column statistics, so its range is estimated from a sample, and the button offers 5.976. The right value is 4.706: Xenium gives the molecules in µm and these cells in pixels of 0.2125 µm, and 1 / 0.2125 = 4.706.

When the pixel size is known, enter 1 / pixel size. When the table is a crop in the cells' own units, keep 1. After adding, the molecules of an epithelial gene should lie on the epithelium; if they are shifted or stretched, add the table again with another Scale.

Explore molecules in the Transcripts tab

Opening the tab shows the molecules, and leaving it hides them again. The genes and settings stay, so returning to the tab brings back the same picture.

The head of the tab gives the number of molecules and genes in the table; the line below it says what is drawn now.

Genes

LayerSelection

Appears when the dataset holds a transcript table.

Add a gene.

Type into Search gene… and click a row. The list is sorted by the number of molecules and shows the first 200 matches; probes for controls carry the word control. Clicking a picked row again removes the gene.

Up to 12 genes.

Each gene gets its own colour in the order picked: green, magenta, cyan, yellow and so on. With 12 genes the other rows are greyed out until one is removed.

The colours are made to stand out on a dark ground, like fluorescent stains, and are not the colours of the Genes tab. They cannot be changed. In the light theme the same hues are drawn darker.

Read the counts.

The list counts all molecules of a gene; the chip counts those that pass Min QV (see Quality). When the view is zoomed out so far that the dots would pile up, only a random sample is drawn, and the chip shows 1:r, one dot for r molecules. Zooming in lowers r until every molecule is drawn and the ratio disappears.

Remove genes.

× on a chip removes that gene, and Clear genes removes all of them. A single gene cannot be hidden or recoloured, only removed.

Show all transcripts

Layer

Appears when the dataset holds a transcript table.

Turn it on.

Show all transcripts draws every molecule of the table as a grey haze. Picked genes are left out of the haze and drawn in colour on top; control probes are left out.

Read it.

The haze is a sample as long as the view is zoomed out: the status line names the level and the ratio, such as 1:386, and says every molecule once zoomed in far enough.

It shows where the tissue holds RNA at all, which helps to tell the absence of a gene from an empty area.

Style

View

Appears when the dataset holds a transcript table.

The display controls sit below the gene list and apply to every gene.

Glow or Crisp.

Glow draws each dot with a soft halo of its own colour; Crisp draws plain discs. Glow is easier to see at a glance; Crisp keeps neighbouring molecules apart. Below a size of 1.5 px Glow is switched off, and the status line says so. Left: Glow; right: Crisp.

Size and Intensity.

Size sets the dot radius on screen, from 0.5 to 6 px; the dots keep this size at every zoom. Intensity, from ×0.25 to ×4, makes the dots stronger or fainter and lets more or fewer of them pile up before a sample is drawn.

Zoom.

Zoomed out, the halos fade first, then a random sample is drawn, so that a dense gene does not merge into a solid patch. Zoomed in, every molecule is drawn and the halos come back.

Cells

View

Appears when the dataset holds a transcript table.

Show, Dim or Hide.

Dim, the default, draws the cells faint and grey, so no cell-type colour competes with the dots. Show keeps their colours; Hide leaves only the molecules. Hovering names the cell under the pointer in every mode.

Over an image.

With an image on, Hide shows the molecules on the stain alone. The Image tab has its own Show, Dim and Hide, and both apply together (see Images).

In the picture, Apoa4 lines the epithelium over the DAPI-stained nuclei, and Defa5 fills the Paneth cells at the crypt base.

The legend.

With Dim or Hide, the legend shows only the gene colours, since the cells' own colours are not on screen. With Show, the gene colours follow the cells' legend.

Quality

Selection

Appears when the table has a quality (QV) column.

Set Min QV.

Molecules with a quality value below Min QV are left out. It starts at 20 and runs from 0 to 40; molecules without a value are always kept.

The counts on the chips follow the slider at once; nothing is loaded again.

Replay sequencing depth

Depth replay asks whether the data are deep enough: how the molecules and the counts per cell would look if only a part of them had been detected.

Analysis

Appears when the dataset holds a transcript table; the curves need the counts of the picked genes in the gene matrix.

Open it.

Pick one or more genes and press Depth replay. A floating panel opens; it can be moved by its head and is closed with ×. Closing it returns the view to 100%.

Play or scrub.

▶ or Space sweeps the depth from 1% to 100%, in 10 s at 1×; ½×, 2× and 4× change the speed. The slider, the arrow keys, Home and End set a depth directly, and clicking or dragging in a chart jumps to that depth. The steps are evenly spaced on a log scale.

Watch the tissue.

At every depth each gene keeps the same random share of its molecules, and a lower depth keeps a subset of a higher one, so scrubbing back and forth shows exactly the same dots. When the view is zoomed out, the screen may already be full and more depth adds no dots: the badge screen full asks to zoom in. Left: 5%; right: 100%.

Read the curves.

Detected is the share of all cells with at least one count; it flattens once every expressing cell is found. Mean count is the average count in those cells and keeps rising. Stability is the correlation between each cell's count at this depth and at full depth; near 1, the map is already there. Genes/cell, from two genes on, is the average number of picked genes a cell detects.

The table under the charts gives the values at the current depth, and the playhead marks it in every chart.

Paint cells.

Paint cells colours every cell by one gene's count at the current depth, on a fixed scale taken from full depth, so the map fades as the depth falls. Hovering a cell gives its count at this depth and at full depth. While the paint is on, Dim is switched to Show.

Badges.

capped: a gene with more than 2 million molecules is loaded as a random 2 million. needs counts: the gene matrix holds normalised values, so the curves cannot be computed. hidden and not drawn: the molecules are switched off or the view does not draw them.

Read the replay as a subsample

Depth replay removes molecules from the existing data at random. When the readouts still change near 100%, deeper sequencing would probably add more; what a deeper run would find cannot be shown.

In the example at 10% depth, Defa5 and Apoa4 already reach a stability of 0.79 and 0.78, while Lgr5, the stem-cell gene with 3,092 molecules, reaches 0.37. Detected still rises for all three up to 100%: more depth would find more expressing cells, but the pattern of the two abundant genes is already set.

Show, combine and capture

Show on any tab.

The Transcripts button in the toolbar switches the molecules on and off over any tab, for example over a gene in the Genes tab. With Cells set to Dim, that tab's colours turn grey too; set Cells to Show to keep them.

Views without molecules.

In 3D, in Embed and in the Expression Landscape the molecules are not drawn; the tab and the button say so. The picture of the dataset on the Home page shows the cells without molecules.

Other layers and tools.

Molecules lie above the cells and the cell boundaries; an image lies below them. Hover, Lasso, Gating and a pinned focus act on the cells, not on single molecules.

Capture.

Capture and figure export include the molecules and add a legend with the gene colours. During a depth replay the legend title gives the depth (see Exporting).

What is kept.

Picked genes belong to the open dataset and are cleared when another one opens. Style, Size, Intensity and Cells carry over to the next dataset. After a page reload, everything starts fresh.

Limits

Up to 12 genes at once. A gene with more than 2 million molecules is loaded as a random 2 million; in the example this is Apoa1, with 3.0 million.

One table per dataset, for 2D data with one section. Scale only, no offset. Single molecules cannot be hovered or selected.
