Pick a feature

  1. Open the multi-modal mouse brain (Spatial-Mux-seq), or any dataset with two or more modalities.

    Modalities are declared at import (see Importing).

  2. Click Multi-omics in the tab rail.

  3. Type a gene in Search features… and click its row.

    Dots mark the modalities that measure it, and an antibody is listed beside its gene, as in Thy1 CD90.2.

  4. Keep Smooth on to compare chromatin.

    Each spot then shows the mean of itself and its 6 nearest neighbours, as single spots are too sparse to compare.

Show the feature in each modality

Each modality is scaled to its own 1st to 99th percentile. Compare where the maps are high, not how bright they are.

Single

Click a modality chip, or press its number key. Hover a spot for the feature’s value in every modality.

The colour bar names the transform: log1p(CP10K) for RNA and chromatin, CLR for protein, which centres near zero.

Side by side

Press Side by side for one map per modality in a floating panel. Hover a map to ring the same spot in every map; click a map to paint that modality.

Correlation of the maps compares each pair: Pearson r across spots, or Lee’s L, which is high when two maps are high in the same neighbourhoods.

Blend

Press Blend and choose a modality for R, G and B. Where two are high, their colours add: red and green make yellow, all three make white.

Split

Press Split and choose Left and Right. Drag Divider to move the line across the tissue.

Compare two modalities

  1. Click Analysis and set A and B.

    ⇄ swaps them.

  2. Press Coupling, States or Lead–lag.

    The ? beside them says what the chosen analysis computes.

Coupling

Ranks every gene measured in both A and B by the Pearson r of its A and B maps across spots, with Lee’s L beside it. Det % is the share of spots that detect the gene in A and in B.

Top coupled and Top anti-coupled list the highest and lowest r. Click a gene to load it as the feature.

States

Cuts the feature in A and in B into low, mid and high thirds and paints the nine combinations: A pushes the colour towards orange, B towards blue.

For an open or active mark against a repressive one, the corners are named Active, Repressed, Mixed and Unmarked.

Lead–lag

Paints z(B) − z(A), each map z-scored over all spots. Warm spots are ahead in B, cool spots ahead in A.

For RNA against ATAC, warm means chromatin open while expression is still low.
