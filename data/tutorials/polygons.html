The mouse colon (Xenium 5K) example holds cell and nucleus outlines.

Show the outlines

  1. Press Boundaries and tick cell, nucleus or both.

    A greyed Boundaries button means the dataset has no outlines yet.

  2. Zoom in.

    Outlines appear once cells are a few pixels wide; the whole section stays as points.

Choose Outline, Fill or Both

The Boundaries row of Style appears once a kind is on.

Outline

Each cell is ringed in its colour around its point. Lower Cell Size to shrink the points.

Fill

Each cell is filled in its colour, with no rim. Hover and click still go to the point inside.

Both

Each cell is filled and rimmed in a lighter shade. With nucleus ticked, every nucleus shows inside its cell.

Colour the outlines

An outline takes its cell's colour, so every tab that paints the cells paints the outlines too: cell types, niches, a gene. Focus and gating dim outlines as they dim points.

Add outlines to your data

At import

  1. Open Cell Boundaries in the import wizard.

    The outlines found in your file are ticked (see Importing).

  2. For a label mask, give its path and press Inspect.

    A mask holds one whole number per cell, 0 for background.

  3. Press Convert.

To an open dataset

  1. Press + beside Boundaries.

  2. Give the file's server path and press Inspect.

    A label mask, a SpatialData store or an .h5ad.

  3. Choose a row and set its Kind.

    The line below gives the share of sampled cells inside their own outline; if it is low, correct Scale or Offset and press Check.

  4. Press Add boundaries.

    Add one kind at a time.

One 2D section only

An atlas, a 3D dataset or sections laid side by side cannot hold outlines. The ? beside a greyed + says which.
